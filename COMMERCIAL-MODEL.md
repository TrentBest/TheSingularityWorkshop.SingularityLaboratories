# Commercial Model

Monetization should fund meaningful compute, expert-quality content, and sustained development without making basic scientific literacy inaccessible. The product is access to laboratory capabilities, authoring tools, compute, education, research workflows, collaboration, and specialized instruments—not ownership of scientific truth or a particular virtual city.

## Potential offerings

| Offering | Value |
|---|---|
| Public laboratories | Free demonstrations and introductory experiments |
| Education toolkit | Instructor authoring, class assignments, guided labs, learner workspaces |
| Personal laboratories | Private workspaces, saved configurations, usage allowances |
| Research laboratories | Higher compute budgets, reproducibility, collaboration, datasets, exports |
| Premium simulation sessions | Metered access to expensive or long-running simulations |
| Creator toolkit | Author, validate, publish, license, and maintain laboratory experiences |
| Institutional environments | Managed classrooms, research groups, private deployments |
| Hosted laboratory services | Remote execution, storage, retention, and collaboration |

These are product hypotheses, not commitments to prices, payment providers, or service levels.

## Separate the lab product from its host

A laboratory is the portable product. A host such as The Singularity Workshop or Singularity City is a way to discover, access, or experience it. A city-based laboratory complex may offer spatial presence, social experiences, and premium venue presentation, but the underlying lab definition, experiment capability, and scientific records remain in Singularity Laboratories.

This separation allows the same laboratory to serve a classroom, a research group, a Workshop page, and a city facility without fragmenting its scientific identity or forcing every customer to use the same world.

## Monetization dimensions

Pricing may combine laboratory or instrument access; compute consumption and duration; memory, storage, and retained history; collaboration and private workspace features; educational seats; premium model fidelity; hosted services; and creator licensing or revenue sharing.

Show a clear estimate before a metered run. Users should set budgets, receive usage warnings, and cancel eligible jobs. Failed-run billing must be transparent.

## Trust requirements

- Never market an educational approximation as research-grade.
- Display model version, validation status, and limitations before purchase.
- Separate compute charges from content or access licensing.
- Provide export paths where licensing permits.
- Preserve provenance when experiments are shared or transferred.
- Do not make paid access a prerequisite for basic scientific understanding.

## Marketplace direction

A future creator ecosystem can let researchers, educators, and developers publish versioned laboratories, instruments, experiment templates, and lessons. Publication includes scientific review metadata, dependencies, licensing, supported model versions, and resource estimates.

Marketplace settlement, identity, entitlements, and hosted compute should integrate through ServerApp and Economy boundaries, not embed in scientific models.

## Virtual laboratory rental and preflight campaigns

The long-term commercial product is access to a scientific capability that a customer can reserve and use in a private virtual workspace. The core value is to test assumptions, expose failure modes, and narrow uncertainty before committing to physical prototypes, scarce instrument time, or operational trials.

A rentable offering must disclose whether it is a pure simulation, a model calibrated against physical evidence, a remotely operated physical instrument, or a hybrid service. These categories are not interchangeable. Each listing should expose model/version, validation level, evidence, assumptions, limitations, supported ranges, resource profile, dependencies, safety metadata, and intended use.

A customer journey should progress through discover → evaluate → reserve → prepare → preflight → challenge → analyze → decide → export. A campaign organizes a baseline and deliberate nominal, boundary, sensitivity, tolerance, disturbance, and supported failure scenarios. Every scenario and run retains its exact configuration and version lineage; out-of-domain results are rejected or labeled as exploratory extrapolation.

Reservations, tenancy, identity, access enforcement, payment, metering, and infrastructure isolation belong to trusted platform services and Economy—not to scientific model contracts. A reservation does not grant execution admission, and neither admission nor a reviewer decision certifies physical safety. The handoff to physical testing remains advisory and requires the physical facility's own qualified review and authorization.

See [Virtual Laboratory Service](docs/virtual-laboratory-service.md) for the domain contract, customer journey, campaign model, offering types, and acceptance gates.

## Phases

1. Prove a useful local laboratory.
2. Establish experiment packaging, versioning, reproducibility.
3. Add saved workspaces and hosted execution.
4. Introduce transparent metering and paid private laboratories.
5. Add institutional offerings and creator publishing after governance exists.
