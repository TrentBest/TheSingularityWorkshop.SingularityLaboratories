# Historical and Archival Inquiry Specimen — A Claim Across Sources

**Method family:** Historical / archival research  
**Status:** Teaching and contract-design specimen. The fictional records below assert no historical facts.

## Research question

What can a researcher responsibly claim about a historical instrument or practice when surviving records are incomplete, mediated, translated, or in disagreement?

## Evidence objects

An archival investigation needs more than an experiment definition:

1. **Repository:** holding institution, collection, catalogue policy, access conditions, and stable reference.
2. **Source item:** manuscript, letter, ledger, image, object record, edition, or later transcription.
3. **Source manifestation:** the specific scan, photograph, edition, translation, or transcription consulted.
4. **Provenance event:** creation, custody transfer, cataloguing, digitization, conservation, translation, or correction, with known and unknown dates.
5. **Source assessment:** authorship/attribution, date range, material context, genre, intended audience, completeness, bias, and fitness for a particular question.
6. **Claim:** a bounded proposition with status and scope.
7. **Claim-evidence relation:** supports, contradicts, contextualizes, depends on, or is silent about a claim.
8. **Interpretation:** reasoning connecting sources to a claim, including alternatives and uncertainty.

A source is not simply reliable or unreliable in the abstract. Assess its fitness for a specific claim.

## Worked teaching scenario (fictional)

A learner investigates the fictional “Asterion water clock.”

| Source ID | Fictional source | What it can establish | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| SRC-A | Workshop inventory dated to a broad period | An object with this name was listed | Inventory may copy an earlier list |
| SRC-B | Later technical drawing | A later author represented a mechanism | May be reconstruction, not eyewitness evidence |
| SRC-C | Modern catalogue entry | How a modern institution describes the object | May depend on SRC-A and SRC-B |

The learner proposes:

- **C-01:** An object called the Asterion water clock appears in the inventory.
- **C-02:** The object used the exact mechanism shown in the later drawing.
- **C-03:** The mechanism was common in the period.

C-01 may be supported by SRC-A if the item is accurately transcribed and identified. C-02 is not established merely because SRC-B depicts the mechanism. C-03 requires comparative evidence beyond these records.

The catalogue entry is not independent corroboration if it repeats the inventory and drawing. Record source dependencies so copied claims do not masquerade as multiple independent witnesses.

## Source criticism

Record creator or attributed creator and confidence; creation date or defensible range separately from the consulted copy's date; original language, script, material, and format where known; repository, collection, shelfmark, and access date; edition, translator, editor, transcription, and digitization details; completeness, damage, omissions, and editorial interventions; intended audience, purpose, genre, and institutional context; custody chain and gaps; conflicts of interest and preservation bias; and rights, access restrictions, and cultural protocols.

Keep original wording or faithful transcription distinct from translation and interpretive paraphrase. Attribute translators and editors.

## Claim lifecycle

Version claims rather than overwriting them. A claim may be proposed, supported, contested, weakened, corrected, superseded, or withdrawn. Preserve who changed its status, when, why, and which evidence or interpretation prompted the change. Contradiction is a research result, not a software validation error.

## Ethical and cultural governance

Public availability does not imply unrestricted reuse. Indigenous, sacred, community-held, personally identifying, or otherwise governed knowledge may require consent, access controls, attribution practices, or publication restrictions. Preserve those conditions instead of assuming every source can be copied into an open dataset.

## Acceptance checks

- [ ] Every claim links to specific source manifestations.
- [ ] Source item and consulted manifestation are distinct.
- [ ] Translation, edition, transcription, and digitization provenance are preserved.
- [ ] Dependent sources are not counted as independent corroboration.
- [ ] Contradictory and inconclusive evidence remains visible.
- [ ] Claim-status changes are versioned with reasons.
- [ ] Rights, access, and community-governance conditions travel with the source reference.
- [ ] Fictional teaching material cannot be mistaken for historical evidence.

## Ownership

Singularity Laboratories owns research-workspace source, claim, interpretation, and provenance records. It does not replace archive catalogues or claim ownership of community-held knowledge. Hosts provide access and presentation subject to source governance.
