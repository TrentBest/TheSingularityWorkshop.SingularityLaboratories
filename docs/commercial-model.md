# Commercial Model

## Principle

Monetization should fund meaningful compute, expert-quality content, and sustained laboratory development without making basic scientific literacy inaccessible.

The commercial product is access to laboratory experiences, compute, collaboration, and specialized capabilities — not ownership of scientific truth or misleading claims about accuracy.

## Potential offerings

| Offering | Value |
|---|---|
| Public demonstrations | Free, discoverable experiences that introduce a discipline or model |
| Education laboratories | Classroom-ready labs, guided lessons, instructor controls, and cohort activities |
| Personal laboratories | Private workspaces, saved configurations, and usage allowances |
| Research laboratories | Higher compute budgets, reproducibility tools, collaboration, and exportable results |
| Premium simulation sessions | Metered access to expensive or long-running simulations |
| Creator laboratories | Tools to author, publish, license, and maintain laboratory experiences |
| Institutional environments | Managed classrooms, research groups, private deployments, and administration |

These are product hypotheses, not commitments to a particular price, payment provider, or service-level agreement.

## Monetization dimensions

Pricing can be based on a combination of:
- laboratory or experiment access;
- compute consumption and execution duration;
- memory, storage, and retained result history;
- collaboration and private workspace features;
- educational seats or institutional cohorts;
- premium instruments, model fidelity, and reviewed content;
- creator licensing or revenue sharing.

A clear estimate should be shown before a metered run begins. Users should be able to set budgets, receive usage warnings, and cancel eligible jobs. Failed runs should have transparent billing treatment.

## Trust requirements

- Never market an educational approximation as research-grade.
- Display model version, validation status, and limitations before purchase.
- Separate compute charges from content or access licensing.
- Provide export paths for user-created configurations and results where licensing permits.
- Preserve provenance when experiments are shared or transferred.
- Do not make paid access a prerequisite for understanding basic scientific concepts.

## Marketplace direction

A future creator ecosystem could let researchers, educators, and developers publish versioned laboratories, instruments, experiment templates, and lessons. Publication should include scientific review metadata, dependencies, licensing, supported model versions, and resource estimates.

Marketplace settlement, identity, entitlements, and hosted compute should integrate through platform boundaries such as ServerApp and Economy rather than being embedded in the scientific model layer.

## Phased approach

1. Prove a useful local laboratory experience.
2. Establish experiment packaging, versioning, and reproducibility.
3. Add saved workspaces and hosted execution.
4. Introduce transparent metering and paid private laboratories.
5. Add institutional offerings and creator publishing after governance and support workflows exist.
